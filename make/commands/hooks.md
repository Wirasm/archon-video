Most of these videos are seen in a feed the viewer did not choose. The first second decides whether they stay. An opening has three channels that should work together:

- `first_frame`: what the viewer sees before any word is spoken. Describe a real, filmable shot.
- `overlay`: a short on-screen text, read with the sound off.
- `spoken`: the first spoken line, which opens a question the video will answer.

Write 5 openings, each from a genuinely different angle on the topic, not five phrasings of one idea. Name the angle in a few words in `angle`. Use specifics: a concrete name, number or moment beats a general claim. Promise only what a video of this length can deliver.

Before you answer, read each spoken line out loud in your head. Rewrite any line a person would not say to a friend, and any line that leans on patterns people now recognise as machine-written ("it's not X, it's Y", "in today's world", "let's dive in", stacked rhetorical questions).

Give the openings ids `h1` to `h5`.
